## Things and kinds of things

An ontology is a written-down agreement about what kinds of things exist in some corner of the world, and how those kinds relate. That is the whole definition. Everything after it is tooling and etiquette.

## Classes, instances, properties

A class is a kind. An instance is a thing of that kind. A property is a way two things can be related, or a way a thing can have a value. If you can say these three words without wincing you can read most schemas.

## Where it goes wrong

It goes wrong when the agreement is written for a world nobody lives in. Most real ontologies are a compromise between how the data actually looks and how three people wish it looked[^1].

[^1]: The three people rarely include whoever has to enter the data.

## Why it matters now

Because language models will happily use whatever categories you hand them, and equally happily invent their own if you hand them nothing. Either way you end up with an ontology. The only question is whether you were in the room.
